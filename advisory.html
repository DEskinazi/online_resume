Fractional Chief Knowledge Officer

Knowledge Governance and AI-Readiness Advisory

A Chief Knowledge Officer owns how an organization structures, maintains, governs, and gives access to its knowledge, so that the AI systems built on it draw from something reliable rather than an unmanaged sprawl. Most organizations that need this function do not yet staff it full-time. I provide it on a fractional basis, scoped to a discovery engagement, a framework build, or ongoing advisory.

Over 13 years I have designed governance frameworks, information architectures, and content operating models for enterprise SaaS and AdTech organizations. At Samsung Ads I built the framework for making a 12,000-page content estate ready for AI retrieval, including the classification rules, the quality gates, and a four-level confidentiality model that governs which AI systems are permitted to see what, then handed off the rollout.

I design for adoption rather than compliance. Most of this work happens across Product, Engineering, Design, and Security without authority over the teams whose content is in scope, which means the systems have to be good enough that people use them by choice.

Available for fractional and advisory engagements: knowledge discovery, governance framework design, AI-readiness assessment, and content operating model build.

Capabilities

Knowledge governance operating models

Ownership, intake, publication readiness, and lifecycle rules that hold up across teams that do not report to you.

  • Built the governance framework for 10 business teams at Samsung Ads (a scope that began at 8 and expanded to 10): classification rules, metadata and labeling structure, quality gates, and lifecycle standards, plus the rollout playbook that let the business teams self-administer it.
  • Prepared the documentation team and the business-team liaison for the rollout, then handed off ownership on departure; the business-team champions self-administer, with the documentation team available for questions.
  • Built an earlier governance charter and framework a year prior that stalled on competing priorities, then used the urgency created by AI adoption to get the second version launched.

AI-readiness and RAG governance

Structuring content so retrieval-augmented systems pull from something clean, current, and correctly scoped.

  • Designed the classification and quality-gate system behind Samsung Ads' RAG ingestion: a tiered model where the lowest tier sets minimum inclusion criteria and the highest requires strict template, style, and maintenance commitments.
  • Adapted the medallion architecture pattern, used in engineering for data pipelines, to content governance after recognizing it matched a quality-gates approach already in progress.

Access and confidentiality classification

Deciding what an AI system is allowed to retrieve, and encoding that decision where the pipeline can enforce it.

  • Designed the four-level confidentiality classification (Critical Internal, Confidential-High, Confidential-Low, Public) carried in page properties on every page of the Samsung Ads framework, governing AI and RAG access independently of content quality.
  • Defined the access-gating policy: ingestion eligibility, and the confidentiality ceiling each AI system is permitted to reach, with classification metadata attached so retrieval is filtered by confidentiality, not only relevance. Design work; enforcement in the retrieval pipeline is an engineering build.
  • Established group-based access control for a Samsung Ads engineering leadership space: created the space, configured page-level restrictions, created the user group, and transitioned ownership so departures deprovision automatically.

Content lifecycle and estate management

Keeping a large body of content from decaying: what to archive, merge, or retire, and how to do it without breaking trust.

  • Reduced stale content by 22% across a 64,000-plus page Confluence estate at Samsung Ads, measured against the full estate.
  • Defined the audit methodology: ownership signals (content from departed contributors), freshness thresholds (not updated in two-plus years, not viewed in roughly eighteen months), a tighter window for time-sensitive material, and structural cleanup of duplicates and superseded pages.
  • Built a removal safeguard: a reader-facing notice and a 90-day window before anything comes down, rather than silent deletion.

Knowledge discovery and assessment

Getting to an accurate picture of how knowledge actually moves through an organization before designing anything.

  • Directed an AI-driven documentation gap analysis at Samsung Ads, mining Product Support tickets for missing and outdated content before the Support team had AI tooling (first by scraping Jira content, later through a direct MCP integration), then directing the writing team to close the gaps with Support subject-matter experts.
  • Investigated how content was used and misused across 10 business teams to design classification and lifecycle rules that fit actual behavior rather than an idealized model.
  • Ran structured elicitation and primary research in an earlier competitive-intelligence practice, including direct interviews with industry experts and key opinion leaders.

Cross-functional enablement

Getting governance adopted upstream, in the workflows where content is actually created.

  • Standardized Product Requirements Documents across Product and Engineering through direct work with individual PMs and an opt-in template; adopted by more than half the teams I had influence over.
  • Moved documentation review upstream into the design process, establishing a standing arrangement in which the team reviews microcopy during the design phase, and partnered the documentation team with Design to build a UX content style guide.
  • Participated in Samsung Ads' cross-functional GenAI core team for internal productivity use cases; facilitated and led brown-bag sessions on the evolving AI toolset for European, US, and Indian teams, including non-engineering stakeholders.
